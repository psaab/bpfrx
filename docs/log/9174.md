# #9174 — three bulk-session-sync gaps

Base: `566d132b0a8962bae3943321dde56a4f40a59e24` (`origin/master`).

Three separate gaps let a stale or mistimed peer frame complete, reset or gut a
bulk transfer. They share a file and a review pass; they do **not** share a fix,
and the cells are kept apart deliberately — a single "the standby is synced"
assertion cannot distinguish them and would let two of the three regress
silently.

## Remedy

**V013 — put the incarnation on BulkEnd.** ADD a field, never redefine one:
`sync_bulk.go` appends `localBootIncarnation()` to the end marker (8 → 24 bytes,
the same #2170 length-gated trailing extension BulkStart got in #5084), and the
reader records `bulkRecvIncarnation` on the ACCEPTED BulkStart path and refuses a
BulkEnd whose known incarnation differs from it. Fail open on either side being
un-incarnated. Refusals counted in `BulkEndsDeadIncarnationDropped` and rendered
by `show chassis cluster`.

Why the epoch alone is not enough: `bulkSendNext` restarts at zero when the peer
reboots, so an end marker buffered on a dead boot's still-ESTABLISHED socket can
carry the SAME epoch as the bulk its replacement has just started.

**V015 — the guard must hold between bulks.** The whole decision moves into
`bulkStartStaleLocked(epoch, inc, switched)`. The between-bulks arm requires an
incarnation on the frame, and that is load-bearing rather than a hedge: within a
bulk a wrong refusal costs one re-prime because a transfer is already in flight;
between bulks nothing is in flight, so a wrong refusal is terminal — and a peer
that REBOOTED legitimately restarts its counter lower (#2198 F2). `switched`
separates those, but only when the peer sends an incarnation at all.

**V014 — arm `needColdPrime` on the epoch edge.** `d.wasDisconnected ||
supersededCurrent || epochReboot`, reusing the value already computed above
(`peerEpochRebootLocked` records as it reads, so a second call would compare
against the baseline the first just advanced).

Every guard here refuses something, so the load-bearing rows are the ones that
must still be **ACCEPTED**. Four of them are: a current-boot BulkEnd, a legacy
8-byte BulkEnd, the next bulk, and a rebooted peer's lower epoch. "Consistency"
is satisfiable by refusing everything, and those four are what makes that
implementation fail.
