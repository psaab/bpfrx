## What the boundary is, and why `<=` is a contract

`admitted_with` admits when the candidate total is `<=` the budget on all three
axes. That is the **exact-fit contract**: it must agree with the Go gate
(`SourceNATAggregateOverBudgetPools` refuses on `cand > Max`, i.e. admits on
`cand <= Max`), so a config whose aggregate lands exactly on a budget has to
install rather than fail closed. Narrowing any axis to `<` puts the two planes in
disagreement about the same config — silently, since neither errors.

## The witnesses, and why each lands exactly on its ceiling

A fixture at `max - 1` passes under both `<` and `<=` and witnesses nothing; one
at `max + 1` is refused by both. **Only `== max` discriminates.**

| witness | budget under test | fixture | charge |
|---|---|---|---|
| `exact_fit_on_the_addresses_budget_installs_7032` | `max_addresses = 8` | one `/29` pool | 8 addresses — exactly on |
| `exact_fit_on_the_port_capacity_budget_installs_7032` | `max_port_capacity = 80` | one `/29` pool × 10 ports | 80 slots — exactly on |

Each asserts **both halves** — exact fit installs, exact fit plus one refuses —
so "admit everything" cannot pass either. Each also asserts the admitted pool
actually built its allocator (`debug_occupancy_words() > 0`), because
`pool_failure == None` alone would pass for a pool that installed in name only.

**They cannot be merged into one fixture.** Port capacity is `addresses ×
ports`, so the exact fit is different arithmetic on each axis: 8 addresses
against `max_addresses = 8`, versus 8 × 10 = 80 slots against
`max_port_capacity = 80`. One fixture cannot sit on both ceilings at once
without also sitting on the pool-count one.

**Single-axis by construction, and asserted rather than assumed.** Each test
gives the two axes NOT under test several orders of magnitude of headroom and
then CHECKS that headroom in an assertion, so a later budget edit cannot quietly
turn one of them into a compound witness.
