# #8939 — the flat-set chain is a COMPILER property, not a grammar defect

- **Timestamp**: 2026-09-05
  - **Action**: measured #8939's proposed remedy against the product and
    **refuted it**, then landed the differential instrument that produces the
    real population.

    The issue filed the over-deep flat-set chain as a GRAMMAR defect and
    proposed rejecting such a `set` command at parse time. Measured on the same
    grammar shape, one command each:

    ```
    applications application myapp protocol tcp destination-port 8080
       packed  proto="tcp" dport="8080"     <- compiler WALKS the chain (#6524)
       split   proto="tcp" dport="8080"

    interfaces gr-0/0/0 unit 0 tunnel source A destination B
       packed  src="10.0.0.1" dst=""        <- compiler does NOT walk
       split   src="10.0.0.1" dst="10.0.0.2"
    ```

    Identical nesting, opposite outcome. The discriminator is the CONTAINER'S
    COMPILER, so a parse-time rejection would reject a spelling that a large
    minority of containers handle CORRECTLY today — `applications` among them,
    which has a test file (`compiler_application_chained_leaves_6524_test.go`)
    devoted to the chained form working.

    Population, by compiling packed and split and comparing rather than by a
    schema predicate: **40 containers LOSE the second leaf, 41 WALK it**, 2
    vacuous, 50 unmeasured. All 40 losers lose the second leaf ENTIRELY — total
    silent loss, never partial corruption.

    Two controls make those numbers mean something. A **vacuity control** on the
    WALK side (both forms compiling to the empty config proves nothing) cut 43 to
    41. A **total-loss discriminator** on the LOSE side (does packed equal
    leaf-A-alone?) is immune to whether leaf B takes a value — which the schema
    cannot tell me, as `schemaNode` has no such field.

    The first predicate I wrote was a schema-shape one, and its own positive
    control killed it: it detected **2 of the 4 examples the issue itself
    names**, while flagging hundreds of working lines across the suite. Its
    "0 of 711 shipped set-lines affected" was a real measurement of a predicate
    that answered the wrong question. The differential instrument replaced it
    because it uses the compiler as the oracle rather than a claim about shape.

    Mutation: the fixture moves in BOTH directions (a dropped row reports
    `+ NEWLY LOSING (regression)`, an added row reports `- no longer losing
    (fixed)`), and — the one that matters — mutating PRODUCTION so
    `applicationDirectLeaves` returns only immediate children kills the WALK
    control and its vacuity check together.

- **Timestamp**: 2026-09-05 (later)
  - **Action**: strengthened the ratchet to three leaves after lane-8388
    demonstrated it could not distinguish the correct fix from a recursive
    descent — and in doing so found my own published WALK count was inflated.

    **The instrument defect lane-8388 found.** At two leaves the flat-set chain
    is indistinguishable from ordinary nesting:

    ```
    two    [address 192.0.2.1] > [external-interface ge-0/0/0]
    three  [address 192.0.2.1] > [external-interface ge-0/0/0 local-address 10.0.0.1]
    ```

    At three the remainder packs onto ONE node's Keys, so a recursive descent
    recovers leaf B and silently drops C — and the two-leaf ratchet records
    that as "no longer losing". The acceptance signal I handed them would have
    certified a wrong fix. Now synthesizes three leaves where a container
    declares three, and classifies each loser `total` / `partial(descent-
    shaped)` / `differs`.

    **What the change then exposed in my own numbers.** Going from two leaves
    to three moved containers from LOSE to WALK — `schedulers scheduler <s>
    friday` among them — which cannot happen if the measurement is sound,
    because a third leaf can only expose more loss. Cause: the two spellings
    compared equal because the LAST LEAF IS NOT OBSERVABLE in the typed config
    at all. The old vacuity control only caught a wholly-empty config.

    With the observability control added:

    ```
    published (2-leaf):  40 losers  41 walked   2 vacuous  50 unmeasured
    corrected (3-leaf):  34 losers   5 walked  42 vacuous  52 unmeasured
    ```

    **So "41 containers genuinely walk the chain", which I published in PR
    #8999 and on #8939, was wrong.** 40 of those 41 were vacuous rows. Worse,
    `applications` — the witness the whole argument rests on — was never in
    the population: its match leaves are `multi` or carry children, so the
    eligibility predicate excludes them. The count never had anything to say
    about the claim it was quoted to support.

    The argument against #8939's parse-time rejection is unchanged and always
    rested on the `applications` control, which drives a real value through a
    real compile and asserts it (`dport == "8080"`). The census corroborated
    nothing. The `walked < 20` floor I had written fired and said exactly
    that; it now rests on the control instead.

    Mutation: reverting to two leaves reds the fixture. Dropping the
    observability control did NOT red it at first — removing it only shuffles
    rows between `vacuous` and `walked`, which the loser-only fixture cannot
    see. The four counts are now part of the fixture, which is what makes it a
    control rather than a comment; the mutant then reports `counts moved, now:
    walked=45 vacuous=2` against `were: walked=5 vacuous=42`.
