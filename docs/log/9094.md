# #8921's ratchet keyed on a COUNT and could not see a SUBSTITUTION

- **Timestamp**: 2026-09-05
  - **Action**: converted the multi-site admission registry from `pair -> count`
    to `pair -> site paths`, after lane-8388 falsified the same design in two
    of their own ratchets and asked whether mine had it. It did.

    #8921 exists to record WHICH schema sites an admission is effective at —
    the finding is that a pair adjudicated at one site is live at every site
    declaring that keyword. **A count cannot see a substitution.** A pair that
    loses one declaring site and gains another holds its count, the ratchet
    stays green, and the thing the issue is about has changed completely.

    Their general form, which is the one to keep:

    > A ratchet keyed on a COUNT cannot see a SUBSTITUTION, and the direction
    > that looks like nothing happened is the one nobody audits.

    Mutation, and it is the whole argument: swap one site in a recorded row
    while holding the count.

    ```
    count form       ok
    membership form  address-set address: SUBSTITUTION (still 2 sites, different ones)
                         + security/address-book/global/address-set
                         - security/SOMEWHERE-ELSE/address-set
    ```

    The registry is now 166 pairs with their site lists, and it is
    self-documenting as a side effect: a reader can see where an admission
    lands without re-running the walk. `UPDATE_8921=1` regenerates, because the
    format change cannot be hand-migrated across 210 rows.

    **Context for why this arrived now.** lane-8388 hit the same class twice in
    one hour from the other direction: a merged fix (#9077) deleted a different
    instrument's container recovery at 24 sites, and the #8807 ratchet reported
    it as `unmeasured rows FELL — GOOD FAILURE, set the constant`. Doing what
    the ratchet said would have banked the coverage loss as progress. Their
    rule from that one belongs beside this:

    > A ratchet's GOOD-NEWS branch is the one that needs the attribution check.
    > A regression announces itself; a coverage loss arrives as an improvement,
    > and the ratchet cannot tell you which because the count moves the same
    > way for both.
