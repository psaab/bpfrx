fingerprint difference tells you something was lost, not what the loss did), and
the obvious reading of `flow aging` was that the elision **disables aggressive
session aging** — its three leaves compile to 0 and the schema documents
`0 = disabled`. That was written into the scope comment and the commit message.

accepted-but-inert advisory.

    dataplane ages sessions on their per-session idle timeout only and does not
    enforce early-ageout / high-watermark / low-watermark pressure-based shedding

**The feature is inert either way.** The elision costs the ADVISORY, not
behaviour — the operator loses the notice telling them their aging config does
nothing. That is a real loss and a smaller one than I had written, and the
ratio guard is what caught the overstatement. It is registered in the
not-read set (4 of 51 now).
