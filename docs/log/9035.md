# 9035 — the stop budget: establish the ordering constraint, then bound the drain

## The instruction that shaped this

> The question is not only "make it faster" but "what must be true before the
> budget is gone", so establish the ordering constraint before the concurrency
> change.

That is the whole fix, and it is why the concurrency change is NOT what landed.

## What must be true before the budget is gone

`TimeoutStopSec=20`, and systemd SIGKILLs there wherever the teardown has got
to. Exactly **two** actions must have completed by then, because only they are
FAIL-CLOSED:

1. **`rg_active` cleared** — this node stops forwarding.
2. **the Kea units stopped** (#6787) — it stops answering DHCP.

Everything else in the teardown is best-effort cleanup whose loss costs
telemetry, not correctness. Miss either and the peer promotes onto a segment
this node is still serving: duplicate OFFERs from two lease databases with
neither aware of the other — exactly what #6787 exists to prevent.

## Why the ordering fix and not the concurrency fix

The issue's acceptance offered either. Bounding the drain is **necessary but
not sufficient, and never could be**: it fixes the one subsystem that happened
to be measured and leaves the next slow one to re-break the same invariant.

The evidence that this is the real shape is in the code's own history. The
fail-closed block already carried the right intent in its own comment — *"clear
rg_active BEFORE stopping subsystems (VRRP, sync) that may hang"* — but it was
positioned relative to the two subsystems anyone had worried about. So every
teardown added ABOVE it over time (telemetry, feeds, RPM, SNMP, FRR, LLDP)
silently moved ownership-release later in the budget, with nothing to notice.
By `9bb0e8f11` it sat ~90 lines below the telemetry joins.

**Ordering makes the invariant structural**: nothing added after that point can
push the fail-closed actions past the budget, because they have already
happened. Both were moved to immediately after `stop(); wg.Wait()`, ahead of
every teardown that can block.

## The constraint that stops it moving even earlier — and where it came from

It cannot precede `stop(); wg.Wait()`. `desired = clusterPri || allVrrpMaster`
(`rg_state.go`), so a live reconcile goroutine observing `desired=true` would
re-drive `rg_active` back to TRUE after this clear — the **#6530 retry doing
exactly its job** against a clear it cannot distinguish from a spurious revert.
The goroutines must be joined first for the clear to stick.

That is the same mechanism I established for #9120 earlier today, arriving from
the other direction: there it explained why a fence does not hold, here it
bounds how early ownership can be released.

**Residual, stated rather than left implicit:** `wg.Wait()` is itself unbounded.
Nothing in this issue's evidence points at it, and bounding a join whose
goroutines are context-cancelled is a different change with a different risk.
Recorded, not folded in.

## And the drain IS bounded, because the two fail differently

- Without the ordering, a bounded drain still delays ownership release by its
  bound times however many exporters exist.
- Without the bound, one wedged generation still burns the remainder of the
  budget after the fail-closed actions.

`telemetryDrainBudget` (3s) + `joinWithBudget` replace the untimed
`flowWg.Wait()` / `ipfixWg.Wait()`. The constant is deliberately NOT tuned to
today's cardinality — the issue's acceptance rules that out explicitly ("a bare
constant drifts the moment `collectorWriteTimeout` or the unit changes"); it is
sized as the share of the budget telemetry may spend at most, which is what
makes the drain **count-independent**. Hitting the bound loses the final flush
for collectors not yet written, which is the correct trade at shutdown.

## Mutation matrix — and the arm that SURVIVED first time

Each mutant `go build`-checked; restores verified byte-identical.

| # | Mutation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | move the fail-closed block back below the telemetry joins | **FAIL** (ordering cell) |
| M2 | restore the unbounded `flowWg.Wait()` | **SURVIVED**, then FAIL — see below |
| M3 | shrink the budget to 1ns (instant give-up) | **FAIL** (bounded + control cells) |
| M4 | unwire only the IPFIX leg | **FAIL** (call-site cell) |

**M2 survived the first scoring, and the reason is a rule I have applied all
day and violated in my own cell.** `TestTelemetryJoinIsBounded9035` exercises
`joinWithBudget` DIRECTLY, so deleting the call site left it green — the cell
still reached the helper by another route. **A bounded helper nothing calls is
the same as no bound.** Fixed by adding
`TestExporterTeardownsJoinThroughTheBudget9035`, which binds the CALL SITES:
both teardowns must join through the budget and neither may carry a bare
`Wait()` on its generation WaitGroup. M2 re-scored: reds with both messages.
M4 then confirms the sibling leg is independently bound, which is the
"fixed one, forgot the other" shape.

Every cell checks its FIXTURE before asserting (the expected call sites were
found, the teardowns were found by name), because a renamed function would
otherwise make the ordering assertion vacuously true — a clean board for a file
the guard never examined.

- `make cluster-deploy` then `make test-failover` — the smoke reboots fw0, so

## Not done

No cardinality cap, and no parallel drain. The cap is what the acceptance
warns about ("only acceptable if mechanically tied to the sum of all remaining
serial deadlines"), and with the ordering fix in place neither is load-bearing
for the failure this issue is about — they would make the drain faster, not
make the invariant hold.
