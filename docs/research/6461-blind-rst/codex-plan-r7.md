### 7. HIGH — Trust decay and silent re-baselining lose both freshness and the named idle-flow target

A totally idle flow’s sequence anchor remains exact; time alone does not make it stale. Nevertheless, v7.2 decays it after about four seconds (`plan@747aa7075:1363-1383`). BGP, SSH, and management flows with longer keepalive intervals therefore spend most of their lifetime in Phase-1 refuse mode, contradicting the claim that Phase 2 specifically protects them.

Decay is not mechanically specified: neither the 40-byte anchor nor 50-byte payload carries refresh time, and `close_seq_plausible` has no `now` input. The current wheel schedules only `last_seen + expires_after`, so a 300-second entry is not visited at four seconds ([expire.rs:38](/home/ps/git/kimi-xpf/.claude/worktrees/6461-research-blind-rst/userspace-dp/src/session/expire.rs:38), [expire.rs:166](/home/ps/git/kimi-xpf/.claude/worktrees/6461-research-blind-rst/userspace-dp/src/session/expire.rs:166)). Decay must be a stored lease evaluated lazily during validation or use a separate timer; stale reconnect bulk must not revive it.

Silent re-baselining also has a terminal case: if the last observed packet causes the over-threshold reset, the key is consumed and no subsequent packet schedules the promised emission. Retain/requeue is required. Only the current owner should heartbeat-renew an unchanged exact anchor; remote apply, import, materialization, or stale bulk must never renew trust. During an update-loss interval, the stale window remains usable for roughly `T_anchor`; at the documented cap, 4,000 blind guesses have about a 46% chance of one hit before decay.

### 9. MEDIUM — The ACK-stall analysis still overclaims loss immunity

For ordinary loss before the firewall, v7.2’s seq-side repair argument is generally sound: later data is initially near the anchor, and retransmission begins near the old edge, allowing `seq_hi` to walk forward. That confirms the narrow `ack_hi` stall for a completed scaled-window repair.

However, leg 3 is not universally “loss-immune” (`plan@747aa7075:651-680,1198-1201`). While a hole is outstanding, the firewall may have advanced `seq_hi(O)` over later observed data while D’s cumulative ACK remains megabytes behind. If D aborts before repair, its close ACK can lie outside the raw-window leg-3 bound. With `seq_hi(D)` absent or untrusted, the legitimate close soft-refuses. This remains a liveness/table-retention residual rather than a blind bypass, but the documentation and test expectation must say that legs 1/3 recover after repair—not that both are unaffected throughout the loss episode.
